# Work & Brew: Case Study

How I took Work & Brew from an idea in March 2025 to a live product: the research, the decisions, and what we built.

**Live:** [workandbrew.app](https://workandbrew.app) · **Founder & Product Lead:** Denisse Medina Flores ([@codingwithden](https://github.com/codingwithden))

## The problem

**For people who work remotely:** students, freelancers and remote workers lose time café hopping. They walk in and there's no seat, no outlet, or Wi-Fi that drops every ten minutes.

**For independent cafés:** many want laptop customers but have no way to say so, and plenty of good ones just aren't known in their own neighborhood.

## Research

- I spent the first year in the field, interviewing café owners and regulars across NYC.
- I've personally worked out of 300+ cafés, so a lot of the friction came straight from my own days.
- Since 2025 we've marked 20+ NYC cafés as permanently closed. I called the owners, and at least 11 of them told me that not being known locally was a big part of why.

That last finding changed the product: Work & Brew isn't only a finder for users. It's also a way to send real foot traffic to independent cafés.

## Product decisions

| Decision | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Every café is hand-picked and visited by a scout | Trust. A listing means someone actually sat there and worked |
| No customer reviews | Reviews reward the loudest people. Scouts write fair, honest notes instead |
| We never share a café's Wi-Fi password | Out of respect for the café. We'd rather send paying customers than freeloaders |
| Perks are redeemed only in the app | Gives cafés a reason to partner and gives us real usage data |
| Launch partners in every borough we can reach | The product only works if the map feels full where you live |

## How it works behind the scenes

I designed the scout workflow so a small team can vet hundreds of cafés:

1. Cafés come from our master database or from user recommendations.
2. A scout gets assigned on the team map and visits in person.
3. The scout submits notes, photos and videos from the map.
4. A two-step review (In Review, then Visit Completed) before a café becomes an **Official Listing**.
5. Only Official cafés show up on the public map, and it updates live.
